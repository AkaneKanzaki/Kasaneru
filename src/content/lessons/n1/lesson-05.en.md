## Lesson goals

This lesson consolidates all of module one: you practice reading glossed classical texts with the right strategy. There are no new patterns — what is tested is your ability to recognize 〜ぬ/〜ず/〜き/〜けり/倒置/係り結び in authentic texts and match them with the gloss.

## Explanation

The strategy for reading glossed classical texts has four steps. **First**, read the 現代語訳 or gloss to grasp the 大意 — don't charge into the 原文 without a map. **Second**, mark every classical form you have learned: 〜ぬ/〜ず (negation), 〜き (memory), 〜けり (hearsay), 倒置, and 係り結び. **Third**, match each mark with the gloss: make sure the nuance is right, for example ありけり = "they say there was", not just "there was". **Fourth**, re-read the 原文 aloud slowly while feeling its rhythm.

Remember this module's principle: classical forms are for recognition, not production. Your goal is to be a sensitive reader, not a classical writer.

The twenty kanji of this lesson: 纏(テン/まとう), 庵(アン/いおり), 廬(ロ/いおり), 寓(グウ/よせる), 鎧(ガイ/よろい), 兜(トウ/かぶと), 矛(ム・ボウ/ほこ), 戟(ゲキ/ほこ), 弩(ド/いしゆみ), 鏃(ゾク/やじり), 琴(キン/こと), 瑟(シツ/おおごと), 笙(ショウ/しょう), 竽(ウ/う), 簫(ショウ), 鉦(ショウ/かね), 鐃(ドウ・ニョウ/どら), 鈸(ハツ・ハチ), 黝(ユウ/あおぐろい), 垓(ガイ).

## Example sentences

1. 〔文語・kuno〕 昔、都に 心優しき 姫が ありけり。(Long ago, they say, in the capital there was a kind-hearted princess.)
2. 〔文語・kuno〕 姫は 月を 見上げ、思わず 涙を 流しき。(The princess looked up at the moon and involuntarily shed tears — I remember.)
3. 〔文学〕 現代語訳を 先に 読むと 原文が 読みやすくなる。(Reading the modern translation first makes the original text easier to read.)
4. 〔文語・kuno〕 戦に 赴く 武士を、民は 惜しまず 見送りき。(The people generously saw off the samurai heading to war — I remember.)
5. 〔日常〕 古文は 注釈を 頼りに 読むのが コツだ。(The trick to reading classical Japanese is to rely on the annotations.)
6. 〔文語・kuno〕 花ぞ 散りける、春の 夕暮れに。(The blossoms scatter on a spring evening.)
7. 〔文学〕 大意を つかんでから 細部に 進むと 挫折しにくい。(Grasping the gist before the details makes you less likely to give up.)

## Exercises

1. In "思わず涙を流しき", the classical forms are: (a. 思わず = 〜ず, 流しき = 〜き / b. 思わず = 〜ぬ, 流しき = 〜けり)
2. 〜けり in ありけり gives the nuance: (a. hearsay / b. command)
3. Translate into English: 花ぞ散りける
4. The first step in reading a glossed classical text is: (a. reading the gloss/modern translation first / b. memorizing all the vocabulary first)
5. The 係り結び in sentence 6: the particle (ぞ) requires an ending in the (連体形) form.
6. Translate into English: 知らぬ間に夜が明けき。

### Answer key

1. a
2. a
3. The blossoms scatter.
4. a
5. 連体形
6. Before I knew it, night had turned to morning.

## Tips / key patterns

- Always work from the gloss to the 原文, never the other way around. The gloss is a bridge, not a cheat sheet.
- Keep a personal list of the classical forms you encounter with their nuances: ぬ = not, ず = without, き = memory, けり = hearsay.
- When 係り結び appears, circle the particle first (ぞ・なむ・や・か・こそ), then check the sentence ending.

## Summary

- Four-step strategy: read the gloss first, mark the classical forms, match the nuances, re-read the 原文.
- Module 1 consolidation: 〜ぬ/〜ず (negation), 〜き (memory), 〜けり (hearsay), 倒置, 係り結び — all for recognition.
- The goal is to be a sensitive reader, not a classical writer.
