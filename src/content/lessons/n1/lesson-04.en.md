## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can recognize two classical rhetorical devices: 倒置 (word-order inversion) and 係り結び (the classical particle-to-ending agreement rule). Both are for reading recognition only — just recognize the patterns and understand their effect.

## Explanation

**1. 倒置 (inversion).** Formula: move the part of the sentence you want to emphasize to an unusual position, usually at the front or back with a pause. Example: 「花が美しい」→「美しい、花が。」 Effect: emphasis and a jolt of rhythm. This technique survives into modern Japanese: 「すごいね、この景色は。」

**2. 係り結び (introduction).** The classical rule: if a sentence contains the 係助詞 ぞ・なむ・や・か, the sentence ending must be in 連体形; if it contains こそ, the ending must be in 已然形. Example: 「花ぞ散りける」— ぞ requires the 連体形 散りける. Second example: 「月こそ惜しけれ」— こそ requires the 已然形 惜しけれ. You only need to recognize these pairs while reading, not use them in writing.

## Example sentences

1. 〔文学〕 美しい、花が。一面に 咲いている。(Beautiful, the flowers. Blooming everywhere.)
2. 〔日常〕 すごいね、この 景色は。(Amazing, this view.)
3. 〔文語・kuno〕 花ぞ 散りける 春の 夕暮れ。(The blossoms scatter — a spring evening.)
4. 〔文語・kuno〕 月こそ 惜しけれ。(It is the moon that I regret to miss.)
5. 〔文学〕 静かだ、夜は。遠くで 虫が 鳴いている。(Quiet, the night. Insects chirping in the distance.)
6. 〔文語・kuno〕 鳥なむ 鳴きける 朝ぼらけ。(The birds are singing — dawn is breaking.)
7. 〔日常〕 大変だったよ、今日の 試験は。(Tough, today's exam.)

## Tips / key patterns

- 倒置 is easy to spot: there's a pause (a comma or breath break) and the word order feels "reversed". Ask: which part is being emphasized?
- For 係り結び, memorize the pairs: ぞ・なむ・や・か → 連体形, こそ → 已然形. Enough for reading; no need to memorize the conjugations.
- 倒置 may be used in your modern writing in moderation; 係り結び never — that's purely the classical world.

## Summary

- 倒置: reverse the word order for emphasis — 美しい、花が。 Also alive in modern Japanese.
- 係り結び: ぞ・なむ・や・か require a 連体形 ending, こそ requires 已然形. Recognition only.
- Both are rhetorical devices: recognize the pattern, understand the effect.
