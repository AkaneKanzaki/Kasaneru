## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can turn data from two mocks into a targeted 2-week improvement plan: prioritizing gaps with the formula frequency × ease × weight, then executing them with specific, measurable drills.

## Explanation

**Priority formula.** For each error pattern (L47), score 1–3: (a) 頻度 — how often it appears; (b) ease — how quickly it can be fixed (collocations: easy; reading speed: medium; listening nuance: hard); (c) point weight. Priority = a × b × c. Example: mixed-up collocations (3×3×2=18) before 即時応答 nuance (2×1×2=4). Tied scores → pick the bigger weight. Low-scoring ones are scheduled afterward, not ignored.

**2-week plan.** Week 1: 70% targeted drills (one gap = one specific drill, e.g. "20 collocations per day"), 30% SRS review. Week 2: 50% half-mocks to measure, 50% light review. H-7: stop new material — only review, SRS, and one light mock. New material at the last minute only adds anxiety.

**Remeasure.** Each drill has an "exit exam": after 5 days of collocation drills, do 20 timed 語彙 questions. Without numerical evidence = not done.

## Example sentences

1. 〔分析〕 頻度と配点で優先度を決める。(Decide priority from frequency and weight.)
2. 〔計画〕 今週は語彙の集中特訓を行う。(This week, intensive vocabulary training.)
3. 〔計画〕 新規の学習は試験一週間前に止める。(Stop new material a week before the exam.)
4. 〔測定〕 ドリルの成果は数字で確認する。(Confirm drill results with numbers.)
5. 〔継続〕 伸びしろの大きい分野から潰す。(Start with the areas with the most room for growth.)
6. 〔話し言葉〕 全部直そうとすると、結局何も直らないよ。(If you try to fix everything, in the end nothing gets fixed.)

## Exercises

1. Gap A: 3×2×3=18. Gap B: 2×3×3=18. Prioritized: a) A b) B c) whichever weight determines passing more d) both postponed
2. H-7 before the exam: a) cram new material b) only review + SRS + light mock c) stop everything d) pull an all-nighter
3. The "exit exam" of a 5-day collocation drill: a) reread b) 20 timed 語彙 questions c) rest d) switch topics
4. A low-ease gap should: a) be prioritized b) be scheduled after easy gaps c) be ignored forever d) be crammed overnight
5. True or false: a good plan fixes all gaps at once.
6. A 1-page plan contains: a) determination b) gap → drill → schedule → exit exam c) target score only d) sleep schedule

### Answer key

1. c — tied scores; the next tiebreaker is weight.
2. b — stop new material; protect your condition.
3. b — improvement proven with numbers.
4. b — do the easily fixed ones first.
5. False — focus on a few priority gaps.
6. b — one executable page.

## Tips / important patterns

- Write your plan on one page: gap → drill → schedule → exit exam.
- Review every 3 days: stalled 進捗 = the drill is wrong, not you being lazy. Change the drill.

## Summary

- Priority = frequency × ease × weight; work from the highest score.
- 2-week plan: week 1 70% drills, week 2 50% measuring; H-7 stops new material.
- Each drill has a numerical "exit exam"; without evidence = not done.
- At most a few priority gaps; the rest queue up.

## Tasks & SRS notes
