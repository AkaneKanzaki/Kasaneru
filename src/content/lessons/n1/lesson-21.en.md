## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use two emphatic N1 patterns: 〜たりとも ("not even a single one") for total rejection that is always followed by a negative form, and 〜だに／〜だにしない ("not even …") for stating something that is not even imaginable. Both are typical of formal written language and often appear in editorials and news reports.

## Explanation

**1. 〜たりとも (pattern 1).** Formula: noun (usually a numeral + counter) + たりとも + negative form. Meaning: "not even a single one" — total rejection with no exceptions.

- 一人たりとも残さない。(Not a single person is left behind.)
- 一円たりとも無駄にしない。(Not a single yen is wasted.)

This pattern only attaches to nouns and the sentence must be negative. Common collocations: 一人たりとも, 一言たりとも, 一度たりとも, 一歩たりとも.

**2. 〜だに／〜だにしない (pattern 2).** Formula: noun + だに（しない）. Meaning: "not even …" — stating something that is not even imagined or has not happened in the slightest.

- 想像だにしない結末だった。(It was an ending that was not even imaginable.)
- 誰も予想だにしなかった。(No one even imagined it.)

In classical literary language, だに also attaches to the dictionary form of verbs: 考えるだに恐ろしい (even just thinking about it is frightening). The form だにせず (without しない) appears in very formal texts.

Distinguish: 〜たりとも rejects existence ("not a single one exists"), 〜だに rejects a possibility in the mind ("not even imagined").

## Example sentences

1. 〔報道〕 避難所では、一人たりとも置き去りにしない方針だ。(At evacuation shelters, the policy is to leave not a single person behind.)
2. 〔報道〕 この法案に例外は一人たりとも認められない。(For this bill, not a single exception is recognized.)
3. 〔報道〕 犠牲者を一人たりとも出さないよう、対策を徹底する。(So that not a single victim falls, measures are tightened.)
4. 〔文語〕 彼は想像だにしない結末を迎えた。(He met an ending that was not even imaginable.)
5. 〔文語〕 かつて誰も予想だにしなかった事態である。(This is a situation that no one even imagined before.)
6. 〔文学〕 思い出すだに胸が痛む。(Even just remembering it, my chest aches.)
7. 〔日常〕 彼が謝るだになんて、想像もできなかった。(I couldn't even imagine him apologizing.)

## Tips / key patterns

- 〜たりとも only attaches to nouns and the sentence must be negative. If the sentence is positive, the pattern is wrong.
- 〜だにしない is often used with words like 想像, 予想, 期待 — things related to thought and expectation.
- Don't confuse it with 〜さえ／〜すら ("even"): 〜さえ can be used in positive sentences, while 〜たりとも and 〜だに(しない) are tied to rejection or absence.

## Summary

- 〜たりとも (noun +): "not even a single one" — total rejection, the sentence must be negative.
- 〜だに／〜だにしない (noun +): "not even …" — something not even imaginable.
- Literary form: dictionary-form verb + だに, e.g. 考えるだに恐ろしい.
- Kanji: the 二十八宿 group — 角亢氐房心尾箕, 斗牛室壁, 奎婁畢, plus the introductory 想祇当芎地黄.
