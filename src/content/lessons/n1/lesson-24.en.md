## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use two N1 "pinnacle" expressions: 〜の至り for feelings that reach an extreme (typical of letters and formal speeches), and 〜の極み for the highest point of something (positive or negative). Both are must-master because they often appear as multiple-choice questions demanding precise nuance.

## Explanation

**1. 〜の至り (pattern 1).** Formula: noun (usually a na-adjective or a feeling) + の至り. Meaning: "the pinnacle of …" / "truly …" — expressing a feeling that has reached an extreme. Very formal, typical of official letters and speeches.

- 光栄の至りです。(It is truly an honor.)
- 恐縮の至りです。(I truly feel obliged.)
- 若気の至りとはいえ、許されない。(Even if it was youthful indiscretion, it cannot be forgiven.)

Standard expressions you must memorize: 光栄の至り, 恐縮の至り, 感激の至り, 若気の至り, 不徳の至り.

**2. 〜の極み (pattern 2).** Formula: noun + の極み. Meaning: "the highest point / climax of …" — usable for positive or negative things, and not limited to feelings.

- 感激の極みであった。(It was truly moving.)
- 贅沢の極みを尽くした。(They displayed the height of luxury.)
- 無礼の極みだ。(It's the height of rudeness.)

Distinguish: 〜の至り is almost always about the speaker's feelings (光栄, 恐縮, 感激) and sounds self-deprecating or praising; 〜の極み is broader — it can describe things, qualities, or situations (贅沢の極み, 皮肉の極み), and is often used for sharp negative judgments.

## Example sentences

1. 〔ビジネス〕 このような賞をいただき、光栄の至りです。(Receiving an award like this is truly an honor.)
2. 〔ビジネス〕 ご迷惑をおかけし、恐縮の至りです。(Having caused you trouble, I truly feel obliged.)
3. 〔文語〕 若気の至りとはいえ、許されることではない。(Even if it was youthful indiscretion, it is not something that can be forgiven.)
4. 〔文語〕 彼の親切は感激の極みであった。(His kindness was truly moving.)
5. 〔文学〕 贅沢の極みを尽くした晩餐だった。(It was a banquet displaying the height of luxury.)
6. 〔日常〕 彼の冗談は失礼の極みだ。(His jokes are the height of rudeness.)
7. 〔文語〕 ご厚意に接し、感激の至りです。(Receiving your kindness, I am truly moved.)

## Exercises

1. Choose the correct one: お招きいただき、___です。(光栄の至り / 光栄の極み)
2. Choose the correct one: 彼の態度は無礼の___だ。(至り / 極み)
3. Fill in with the correct pattern: 若気___とはいえ、責任は取らねばならない。
4. Translate into Japanese (formal): "It is truly embarrassing." → ______
5. 〜の至り is more often used for: (extreme personal feelings / the highest level of a thing).
6. Arrange into a correct sentence: 至り / 恐縮の / です → ______

### Answer key

1. 光栄の至り
2. 極み
3. の至り
4. 赤面の至りです。
5. extreme personal feelings
6. 恐縮の至りです。

## Tips / key patterns

- Memorize the standard expressions as packages: 光栄の至り, 恐縮の至り, 若気の至り — all three always use 至り, not 極み.
- Question hint: if the context is an official letter or a humble feeling → 至り; if it's a sharp judgment or the object is a thing/situation → 極み.
- 〜の極み can sound negative (無礼の極み, 皮肉の極み), while 〜の至り is almost never used to criticize.

## Summary

- 〜の至り (noun + の): "pinnacle of feeling" — formal, for extreme feelings, typical of letters and speeches.
- 〜の極み (noun + の): "highest point" — broader, positive or negative, usable for things and situations.
- Standard expressions: 光栄の至り, 恐縮の至り, 若気の至り vs 贅沢の極み, 無礼の極み.
- Kanji: 筆備前俵隈取得謡輿寄屋単簪櫛独凧羽板碁将.
