## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can read Japanese newspaper headlines: recognizing the newspaper-specific passive pattern (particles are often dropped), and breaking down headlines with the who–what–how method. You will also master 8 kanji of the newspaper-language module.

## Explanation

**1. The basic passive-headline form.** The pattern: noun + 、+ passive verb (+ closing). The most common closings are 見通しだ ("expected"), 予定だ ("scheduled"), and ことになった ("it has been decided").

- 法案、可決される見通しだ (The bill is expected to be passed.)
- 会見、延期されることになった (It was decided that the press conference would be postponed.)

Note: there is no は, が, or を. The comma 、 replaces those particles.

**2. How to read: who–what–how.** Each headline answers three questions: WHO (the actor/topic, often omitted), WHAT (what happened), HOW (its status: already happened / expected / decided). Example: 「容疑者、昨夜逮捕される」→ who: 容疑者 (suspect), what: 逮捕 (arrest), how: される (was arrested — past).

**3. Passive without an actor.** In newspapers, the actor is almost never written. Your focus should be on the result of the action, not who did it. Compare: ordinary sentence 警察が容疑者を逮捕した vs headline 容疑者、逮捕される.

The eight kanji of this lesson: 覇 (は = hegemony, e.g. 覇権), 翌 (よく = next, e.g. 翌日), 曰 (いわ = it is said, e.g. 曰く), 様 (よう = appearance, e.g. 様々), 海 (うみ, e.g. 海洋), 洋 (よう = ocean, e.g. 海洋), 笑 (わら = to laugh, e.g. 笑い), 汐 (しお = tide).

## Example sentences

1. 〔新聞〕 法案、可決される見通しだ。(The bill is expected to be passed.)
2. 〔新聞〕 新製品、来月発表される。(A new product will be announced next month.)
3. 〔新聞〕 議員選挙、来年行われる。(The legislative election will be held next year.)
4. 〔新聞〕 台風、あす九州に近づく見通し。(The typhoon is expected to approach Kyushu tomorrow.)
5. 〔新聞〕 地震の被害、拡大するおそれ。(There are fears the earthquake damage will spread.)
6. 〔新聞〕 容疑者、昨夜逮捕される。(The suspect was arrested last night.)
7. 〔新聞〕 地域の祭り、来年も開かれる。(The regional festival will be held again next year.)

## Practice

1. Turn into a headline: 新製品を 発表する → ______
2. Fill in the blank: 法案、______見通しだ。(passed)
3. Choose the correct headline: a. 法案が 可決される見通しだ / b. 法案、可決される見通しだ
4. Translate into a headline: "The election will be held next year." → ______
5. Break down this headline (who–what–how): 首相、来週訪日する見通し
6. Fix into headline style: 台風が あす 近づく → ______

### Answer key

1. 新製品、発表される
2. 可決される
3. b
4. 選挙、来年行われる。
5. Who: 首相; what: 訪日 (visit); how: 見通し (expected)
6. 台風、あす近づく(見通し)

## Tips / key patterns

- 曰く (いわく) means "it is said" — a quotation marker typical of newspapers: 彼は「無理だ」と曰く.
- 覇権 (はけん) = hegemony; often appears in international headlines: 地域の覇権を目指す国.
- Distinguish 見通しだ (a forecast, which may miss) from 予定だ (an official plan) and ことになった (a final decision).
- JLPT trap: a headline without an actor is not an active sentence — don't translate it as "the bill passes (something)".

## Summary

- Passive headline: noun + 、+ passive V (+ 見通しだ / 予定だ / ことになった); the particles は・が・を are dropped.
- Read with who–what–how; the actor is almost always omitted.
- 見通しだ = expected; 予定だ = scheduled; ことになった = it has been decided.
- Kanji: 覇 (hegemony), 翌 (next), 曰 (it is said), 様 (appearance), 海・洋 (sea/ocean), 笑 (laugh), 汐 (tide).
