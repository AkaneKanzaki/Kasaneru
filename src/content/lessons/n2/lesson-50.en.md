## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you will assess your N2 readiness from data: the pass criteria (ESTIMATES), a per-strand checklist, the final-7-days plan, and exam-day plan.

## Explanation

**N2 readiness criteria (three conditions, all ESTIMATES — not official JLPT scores):**

1. **Two CONSECUTIVE Real mocks passed** (estimated total ≥90/180). One pass could be luck; two in a row = consistency.
2. **No failed sections:** each section above the estimated threshold (±19/60). One section below threshold = not ready, whatever the total.
3. **Rising/stable trend:** second mock ≥ first (gap ≤5 points). Falling trend = fix it first before moving on.

**Strand checklist** (honest ✓/✗):

- Goi: ≥70% correct; daily SRS smooth.
- Bunpou: no longer confuse the main pattern pairs.
- Dokkai: 500–600 character text finished (read + answer) in ≤8 minutes.
- Choukai: lasts 50 minutes; 4K notes automatic.

**Final 7 days (taper):**

- H-7–H-5: light drills 30'/day for the weakest strand (L47 clinic).
- H-4–H-3: clinic review + SRS; NO new mocks.
- H-2: active rest, light material without a timer.
- H-1: prepare belongings (L48 checklist), sleep 7–8 hours. DON'T study new material.

**Exam-day plan:**

- Wake up 3 hours before session 1; breakfast as usual.
- Arrive 30 minutes early; set up your desk per the L48 discipline card.
- Session 1 (105') → break without notes → session 2 (50').
- Mindset: a question hard for you is hard for most too — skip it, secure what you can.

## Practice

1. Name the three N2 readiness criteria + their score notes.
2. Why is the requirement "two consecutive mocks", not one?
3. What is allowed/not allowed from H-3 to H-1?
4. What's the right mindset when hitting a very hard question on exam day?
5. Fill in your own readiness card.

### Answer key

1. (a) two consecutive Real mocks ≥90/180 (est.); (b) each section > the estimated threshold (±19/60); (c) rising/stable trend. All ESTIMATES, not official JLPT scores.
2. One pass could be luck; two in a row = consistency.
3. Allowed: clinic review, light SRS, active rest. Not allowed: new mocks/material, staying up late.
4. A question hard for you is hard for most too — skip it, secure what you can.
5. Correct example: each row filled honestly + a reasoned READY/NOT YET decision.

## Tips / key patterns

- "Feeling not ready" is normal — trust the data, not your feelings.
- Don't move the exam schedule because of one bad mock — look at the trend of the last three mocks.
- On the H-1 evening, write three things you've already mastered to fight anxiety.
- After the exam, keep all tables and cards — archives for retaking or moving up to N1.

## Summary

- Ready = two consecutive Real mocks ≥90/180 (est.) + no failed sections (±19/60) + rising/stable trend.
- All numbers are ESTIMATES (official scores = statistical scaling).
- Checklist: goi ≥70%, bunpou no confusion, dokkai ≤8'/text, choukai 50'.
- Taper: light drills → review → active rest → H-1 ready. Exam day: arrive early, follow the pacing.
