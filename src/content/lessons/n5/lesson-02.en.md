## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can read and write the first 20 hiragana: the five vowels (あいうえお) plus the K row (かきくけこ), S row (さしすせそ), and T row (たちつてと). You'll also learn simple words that only use those letters.

## Hiragana chart

**Vowel row**

| あ (a) | い (i) | う (u) | え (e) | お (o) |

**K row**

| か (ka) | き (ki) | く (ku) | け (ke) | こ (ko) |

**S row**

| さ (sa) | し (shi) | す (su) | せ (se) | そ (so) |

**T row**

| た (ta) | ち (chi) | つ (tsu) | て (te) | と (to) |

Note three unexpected sounds: し is read "shi" (not "si"), ち is read "chi" (not "ti"), and つ is read "tsu" (a "ts" sound like in "tsunami"). Practice writing each letter with the correct stroke order: generally top to bottom, left to right.

## Tips / key patterns

- Indonesian learners often read し as "si" and ち as "ti". Memorize their sounds: "shi" and "chi".
- Distinguish き (ki) and さ (sa) when writing: their curves go in opposite directions.
- Small つ (tsu) works differently from big つ — the small one marks doubled consonants, which you'll learn later.
- Pronounce each vowel clearly: a, i, u, e, o don't glide like Indonesian diphthongs.

## Summary

- The first 20 hiragana: vowels あいうえお + the K, S, T rows.
- Special sounds: し = shi, ち = chi, つ = tsu.
- Each hiragana represents one syllable (consonant + vowel).
- General stroke order: top to bottom, left to right.
- Reading aloud practice speeds up memorizing the letter shapes.
