## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can catch the main idea of a short email or message in a single read: who is writing, what the invitation is, and what the reader should do. This trains the "purpose of the text" question that always appears in the JLPT.

## Reading text: invitation email

Read the email below. Set a 120-second timer: read it once, then summarize its main idea in one English sentence, and answer the questions.

> 件名：週末の旅行について
> シンタさんへ
> こんばんは、ブディです。
> 今週の土曜日、一緒にボゴールへ行きませんか。
> 朝8時にジャカルタ駅で会いましょう。電車で1時間ぐらいです。
> ボゴールで有名な植物園を見て、昼ごはんを食べましょう。
> 植物園の入場料は一人25000ルピアです。カメラを忘れないでください。
> 帰りは午後4時の電車に乗る予定です。
> 雨が降るかもしれませんから、傘を持ってきてください。
> 返事を楽しみにしています。
> ブディ

## Comprehension questions

1. このメールの主な内容は何ですか。
a. ボゴールへの誘い
b. 植物園の説明
c. 電車の時刻表の案内
2. 二人はいつ、どこで会いますか。Write in Indonesian.
3. ブディさんはシンタさんに何を持ってきてほしいですか。二つ答えなさい。

### Answer key

1. a. ボゴールへの誘い
2. Saturday at 8 a.m., at Jakarta Station.
3. Camera and umbrella (傘を持ってきてください、カメラを忘れないでください).

## How the timed drill works

1. Read the questions first (10 seconds), then read the email in one pass (120-second timer).
2. Close the text. Write the main idea in ONE English sentence. Example of a good answer: "Budi is inviting Sinta to go to Bogor on Saturday."
3. Answer the 3 questions from memory.
4. Score your summary: does it mention who, what, and when? If anything is missing, repeat the drill.

For main-idea questions, the correct answer is almost always at the start of the text (the invitation sentence) — don't be tricked by details in the middle.

## Tips / main-idea strategies

- The first sentence of an email usually states the writer's purpose. That is 70% of the main-idea question's answer.
- Ignore number details (ticket prices, return times) when looking for the main idea — those details are for retrieval questions, not main-idea questions.
- Invitation words like ～ませんか and ～ましょう are strong signals: this text contains an invitation.
- If two answer options look similar, pick the more general one — the main idea is never that detailed.

## Summary

- The main idea of an email is usually in the opening sentences.
- Summarize in one sentence: who + what + when.
- Ignore number and schedule details at first when looking for the main idea.
- ～ませんか / ～ましょう mark an invitation text.
- Practice with a 120-second timer until your summary always mentions who, what, and when.

## Tasks & SRS notes
