## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use the three most basic particles correctly: は for the topic, が to emphasize the doer, and を for the object. You'll also start reading the first three kanji: 人, 日, 本.

## Explanation

### Pattern 1: は vs が

- は marks the **topic**: what is being talked about. わたしはせんせいです — "I am a teacher." (talking about me)
- が marks the **emphasized doer**: used when answering "who" or stressing who does something. だれがせんせいですか。わたしがせんせいです — "Who is the teacher? I am the teacher."

Rule of thumb: introductions and general statements use は. Questions with だれ・なに・どこ and their answers use が.

### Pattern 2: を (object)

を (read "o") marks the **object** of a verb — the thing the action applies to:

`doer は object を verb` — Sinta は本をよみます — "Sinta reads books."

Without を, the sentence loses the target of its action. を always attaches right before the verb.

## Example sentences

1. わたしは Budi です。— I am Budi.
2. だれがせんせいですか。わたしがせんせいです。— Who is the teacher? I am the teacher.
3. Sinta はまいにち本をよみます。— Sinta reads books every day.
4. Budi はみずをのみます。— Budi drinks water.
5. わたしはにほんごをべんきょうします。— I study Japanese.
6. だれがにほんごをおしえますか。— Who teaches Japanese?
7. あの人はインドネシアの人です。— That person is Indonesian.
8. 今日はいい日です。— Today is a good day.

## Tips / key patterns

- A typical Indonesian mistake: using は to answer だれ. Answers to "who" always use が.
- を is read "o", not "wo" — just as the particle は is read "wa".
- The kanji 本 has two common readings: ほん (book) and もと (origin). For N5, memorize ほん first.
- The S–O–V pattern (subject–object–verb) is the reverse of Indonesian; get used to putting the verb at the end.

## Summary

- は = topic marker; が = doer emphasis (especially for answering だれ・なに・どこ).
- を = object marker, always right before the verb.
- New kanji: 人 (ひと, person), 日 (ひ/にち, day), 本 (ほん, book).
- Sentence order: doer は object を verb.
- を and は have special readings: "o" and "wa".
