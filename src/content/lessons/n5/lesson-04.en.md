Katakana is for loanwords, foreign names, and emphasis. Dakuten/handakuten work the same as in hiragana.

## Example sentences with loanwords

1. わたしはコーヒーがすきです。(watashi wa koohii ga suki desu) — I like coffee.
2. Sinta はコンピューターでべんきょうします。(Sinta wa konpyuutaa de benkyou shimasu) — Sinta studies using a computer.
3. レストランはえきのちかくです。(resutoran wa eki no chikaku desu) — The restaurant is near the station.
4. Budi はサッカーがすきです。(Budi wa sakkaa ga suki desu) — Budi likes soccer.
5. わたしはパンをたべます。(watashi wa pan wo tabemasu) — I eat bread.

## Tips / key patterns

- The ー mark lengthens the vowel: コーヒー is read "koohii", not "kohi".
- Watch out for lookalike pairs: シ (shi) vs ツ (tsu), ソ (so) vs ン (n). The difference is the stroke direction.
- Loanwords follow Japanese sounds, not English spelling: "computer" becomes コンピューター.
- ヲ is almost never used in modern Japanese.

## Summary

- Katakana is used for foreign loanwords and foreign names.
- The 46 katakana match the 46 hiragana; only their shapes differ.
- The ー mark lengthens vowel sounds; dakuten/handakuten are the same as in hiragana.
- Loanwords are pronounced with Japanese sounds, not their original English sounds.
