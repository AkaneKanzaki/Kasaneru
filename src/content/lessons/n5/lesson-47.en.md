## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can turn mock results into a repair plan: grouping mistakes per section, finding your weakness patterns, and deciding concrete actions for each category. A score without analysis is just a number; analysis turns it into progress.

## Step 1: gather the data

Take your Study mode mock results (L46). For every question you got wrong OR answered hesitantly, record four things: the question number, the section, the question type, and the suspected cause. The cause is usually one of: didn't know the vocabulary, didn't understand the pattern, tricked by an option, or ran out of time.

## Analysis table template

Copy this table into your notebook and fill it in from your mock results:

| No | Section | Question type | Why wrong | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Vocabulary | kanji reading | forgot the reading of 旅行 | SRS + write 3x |
| 12 | Vocabulary | word choice | tricked by similar option | memorize word pairs |
| 23 | Grammar | particle | unsure は vs が | review L07 |
| 31 | Reading | main idea | ran out of time | drill L42, 120 seconds |
| 40 | Listening | numbers | misheard 4 vs 8 | drill the 四日/八日 pair |

Fill in at least 10 rows from your most frequent mistakes. An honest table is worth more than a neat one.

## Step 2: find the patterns

Look at the "Question type" and "Why wrong" columns, then look for what repeats:

- **Leaking Vocabulary section** → the problem is almost always vocabulary volume. Action: add 10 SRS cards per day from the words you got wrong, and review the related vocabulary lessons.
- **Leaking Grammar section** → go back to the lesson for that pattern (L07–L40). Study one pattern again until you can explain it to someone else.
- **Leaking Reading section because of time** → not an ability problem, a pacing problem. Action: timed drills L41–L42 every day for a week.
- **Leaking Listening section** → distinguish: wrong vocabulary (add SRS) or misheard sounds (distractor drill L44 + play-once protocol L43).

## Example case

Sinta scored 14/20 in Vocabulary. From her table, 4 mistakes were kanji-reading questions and 2 were from being tricked by similar options. The pattern is clear: kanji readings. Her action: 15 SRS cards focused on kanji readings + writing each kanji 3 times while reading it aloud. A week later, her Vocabulary score went up — not because she studied "harder", but because she attacked the right spot.

## Healthy repair rules

1. Fix at most 2 weakness patterns per week. More than that, and nothing gets finished.
2. One repair session = 25 minutes focused on one category, not 2 mixed hours.
3. Re-measure with a half mock (L45) after a week — if the pattern is gone from the table, your repair worked.
4. The same mistake appearing three times in a row means the action is wrong, not that you're stupid. Change the action.

## Summary

- Error analysis = record the number, section, question type, cause, and action in a table.
- Find the repeating patterns; those patterns are your repair targets.
- Actions per section: Vocabulary → SRS; Grammar → review the lesson; Reading → timed drills; Listening → distractor drills.
- At most 2 patterns per week, 25 minutes per session, re-measure every week.
- A mistake repeated three times = change the strategy, not blame yourself.
