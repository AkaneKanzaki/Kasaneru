## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can read short announcements under time pressure and find key information (date, time, place, deadline) without translating word by word. This trains the speed needed for the JLPT Reading section.

## Reading text: announcements

Read the text below. Set a 90-second timer: read it once, then answer the questions without looking at the text again.

> お知らせ
> 日本語スピーチコンテストのお知らせです。
> 日時：12月15日（土）午後2時から
> 場所：さくらホール（ジャカルタ）
> テーマは「私の夢」です。発表の時間は一人5分です。
> 参加したい人は、12月5日までに事務所に申し込んでください。
> 見学だけの人も大歓迎です。友達を誘って、ぜひ来てください。
> 質問がある人は、シンタさんに聞いてください。
> 皆さんの参加を楽しみにしています。
> さくら日本語学校

## Comprehension questions

1. スピーチコンテストはいつですか。
a. 12月5日の午後2時
b. 12月15日の午後2時
c. 12月15日の午前2時
2. 参加したい人は何をしなければなりませんか。
a. 12月5日までに事務所に申し込む
b. シンタさんに電話する
c. 友達を一人誘う
3. スピーチのテーマは何ですか。Write your answer in Indonesian.

### Answer key

1. b. 12月15日の午後2時
2. a. 12月5日までに事務所に申し込む
3. 「私の夢」("My dream")

## How the timed drill works

1. Skim the questions first before reading the text (10 seconds).
2. Start a 90-second timer and read the text in one pass.
3. Close the text and answer the 3 questions from memory.
4. Repeat the drill with other announcement texts in the app until you can answer 3 out of 3 within 90 seconds.

N5 target: one short text + 2–3 questions should be finished in about 2 minutes.

## Tips / fast-reading strategies

- Look for numbers, dates, and times first — that information is easiest to spot at a glance and is often asked about.
- Don't stop at words you don't know. Guess from context and keep moving.
- A word like ください after a long sentence usually contains an important instruction (a deadline, how to register). Note it mentally.
- A common mistake among Indonesian learners: reading from start to finish twice. Read it in one pass only, then answer.

## Summary

- Read the questions first, then read the text — that way you know what information to look for.
- Numbers, dates, times, and places are the main targets for skimming.
- 90 seconds for a short text + 3 questions is a good practice pace.
- The word ください marks important instructions in announcements.
- Speed comes from repeated practice, not from memorizing new vocabulary.
