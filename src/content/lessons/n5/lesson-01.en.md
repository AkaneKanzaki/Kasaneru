## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can greet people according to the time of day (morning, afternoon, evening) and introduce yourself with simple sentences. You can also point at things with これ・それ・あれ.

## Explanation

### Pattern 1: X は Y です

The basic pattern of a polite Japanese sentence:

`noun は noun です` — meaning "X is Y".

Pattern example: 私は Budi です (watashi wa Budi desu) — "I am Budi."

The particle は is read **"wa"** and marks the sentence topic; です at the end makes it polite.

### Pattern 2: demonstratives これ・それ・あれ

- これ (kore) = this — a thing close to the speaker.
- それ (sore) = that — a thing close to the listener.
- あれ (are) = that — a thing far from both of them.

All three are followed by は … です, for example これは本です — "This is a book."

## Example sentences

1. これは日本の本です。(kore wa nihon no hon desu) — This is a Japanese book.
2. 私はインドネシア人です。(watashi wa indonesiajin desu) — I am Indonesian.
3. それは Sinta のペンです。(sore wa Sinta no pen desu) — That is Sinta's pen.
4. あれはモナスです。(are wa monasu desu) — That (over there) is Monas.
5. Budi は大学生です。(Budi wa daigakusei desu) — Budi is a university student.
6. 私は先生です。(watashi wa sensei desu) — I am a teacher.

## Practice

1. Fill in: ___ は学生です。(___ wa gakusei desu) — "I am a student."
   a. これ (kore) b. 私 (watashi) c. それ (sore)
2. The object is in your conversation partner's hand. The correct demonstrative: ___.
3. Arrange into a correct sentence: 本 / です / これ / は
4. Translate into Japanese: "This is a bag."
5. You meet your teacher at 8 PM. The right greeting is ___.
   a. おはようございます (ohayou gozaimasu) b. こんばんは (konbanwa) c. こんにちは (konnichiwa)
6. Translate into Japanese: "I am from Jakarta."

### Answer key

1. b. 私 (watashi)
2. それ (sore)
3. これは本です。(kore wa hon desu)
4. これはかばんです。(kore wa kaban desu)
5. b. こんばんは (konbanwa)
6. 私はジャカルタの人です。(watashi wa jakarta no hito desu)

## Tips / key patterns

- は is always read "wa" when it is a particle. This is the most common mistake among Indonesian beginners.
- Don't use こんにちは at any time: morning = おはようございます, evening = こんばんは.
- The これ・それ・あれ distinction often appears in JLPT N5 in object-position questions.
- です is required at the end of a polite sentence.

## Summary

- The pattern X は Y です means "X is Y"; は is read "wa".
- これ = near me, それ = near the listener, あれ = far from both of us.
- Greetings change with the time of day: morning, afternoon, and evening are different.
- すみません = excuse me / sorry.
- はじめまして is used when meeting for the first time.
