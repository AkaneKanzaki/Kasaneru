## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can group Japanese verbs into three groups and change them into the polite ます form, including the negative ません form. You'll also read the kanji 月, 火, 水.

## Explanation

### Pattern 1: the three verb groups

Look at the dictionary form (ending in う):

- **Group 1:** ends in a -u sound other than the special る, e.g. かう, はなす, かえる. The largest group.
- **Group 2:** ends in -eru or -iru, e.g. たべる, みる, おしえる, おぼえる.
- **Group 3:** only two words — する (to do) and くる (to come). Memorize them by heart.

### Pattern 2: the ます form (+/-)

The ます form = the polite present/future form.

- Group 1: change the final -u sound to -i + ます. かう → かいます, はなす → はなします.
- Group 2: drop る + ます. たべる → たべます, おしえる → おしえます.
- Group 3: する → します, くる → きます.

Negative form: change ます to ません. たべます → たべません ("doesn't eat").

## Example sentences

1. わたしはまいあさごはんをたべます。— I eat rice every morning.
2. Sinta はしんぶんをよみません。— Sinta doesn't read the newspaper.
3. Budi はがっこうにいきます。— Budi goes to school.
4. わたしはテレビをみます。— I watch TV.
5. はははりょうりをつくります。— Mom cooks.
6. わたしはコーヒーをのみません。— I don't drink coffee.
7. ともだちはジャカルタにすみます。— My friend lives in Jakarta.
8. せんせいはにほんごをおしえます。— The teacher teaches Japanese.

## Tips / key patterns

- かえる (to go home) is group 1, not group 2 — don't be fooled by its -eru ending.
- The ます form doesn't distinguish "doing now" and "will do": たべます can mean "eat" or "will eat", depending on the time context.
- Group 2 verbs are few; memorize the common ones: たべる, みる, ねる, おきる, いる.
- In JLPT N5, ます/ません conjugation questions almost always appear — master the pattern.

## Summary

- Group 1: -u → -i + ます. Group 2: drop る + ます. Group 3: します・きます.
- Polite negative: change ます → ません.
- Watch out for fake -eru verbs like かえる (group 1).
- New kanji: 月 (つき), 火 (ひ), 水 (みず).
- The ます form is used for the present and the future.
