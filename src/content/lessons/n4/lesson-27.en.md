## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use special kenjougo verbs to humble yourself when speaking with superiors or outsiders. You understand its direction, opposite to sonkeigo in L26, and can choose the right form for the subject "I".

## Explanation

Kenjougo is humble language that **lowers yourself** to elevate the listener. Its direction is the reverse of sonkeigo: it is used for **your own actions** when dealing with superiors or outsiders.

- いう → **もうす** (to say, to be called)
- する → **いたす** (to do)
- いく・くる → **まいる** (to go, to come)
- いる → **おる** (to be)
- みる → **はいけんする** (to see)
- かりる → **はいしゃくする** (to borrow)
- しる・おもう → **ぞんじる** (to know, to think)
- きく・たずねる → **うかがう** (to ask, to visit)

Compare directly with L26: a superior who comes = いらっしゃる (sonkeigo), I who come = まいる (kenjougo). A superior who says = おっしゃる, I who say = もうす. A superior who sees = ごらんになる, I who see = はいけんする. The pairs always work like this, and JLPT questions love to ask them side by side.

Besides memorization, there is a general pattern: **お/ご + ます-stem + する (or いたす)**. Examples: もつ → おもちする, あんないする → ごあんないする, れんらくする → ごれんらくする. This pattern is for verbs without a special form.

The two kanji of this lesson: 業 (ぎょう = business, work), 株 (かぶ = stock).

## Example sentences

1. わたしは ブディと もうします。(My name is Budi.)
2. しりょうを はいけんしました。(I have looked at the materials.)
3. あした かいしゃへ まいります。(Tomorrow I will come to the office.)
4. ペンを はいしゃくしてもいいですか。(May I borrow a pen?)
5. その けんは ぞんじております。(I am aware of that matter.)
6. ぶちょうに ごいけんを うかがいました。(I asked the section chief for his opinion.)
7. 株(かぶ)の 業(ぎょう)は むずかしいです。(The stock business is difficult.)

## Tips / key patterns

- Memorize the sonkeigo–kenjougo pairs side by side: おっしゃる–もうす, なさる–いたす, いらっしゃる–まいる, ごらんになる–はいけんする.
- もうします is the most frequently used form: it always appears when introducing yourself in formal situations.
- うかがう covers "to ask", "to visit", and "to hear" — all humble versions. Context decides the meaning.
- Test the direction like in L26: if the subject is a superior, kenjougo is definitely wrong. These two lessons lock each other in.

## Summary

- Kenjougo humbles yourself: used for your own actions before superiors/outsiders.
- Memorize the eight special verbs: もうす, いたす, まいる, おる, はいけんする, はいしゃくする, ぞんじる, うかがう.
- General pattern: お/ご + ます-stem + する/いたす (おもちする, ごあんないする).
- Opposite of sonkeigo: don't mix up the direction. This is the heart of keigo questions.
