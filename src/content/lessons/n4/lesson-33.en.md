## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you'll be able to use 〜みたい for subjective impressions from your own observations ("looks like…"), and 〜らしい for widely known characteristics or things you've heard — distinguished through minimal pairs.

## Explanation

〜みたい = "looks like…" — a SUBJECTIVE IMPRESSION from your own observation ("it feels like… to me").

〜らしい = two meanings: "typical of…" (a characteristic known to many people), and "apparently…" (things you've heard, similar to 〜そうだ in L32 but more casual).

Formation:

- みたい: noun + みたい (ともだちみたい), verb plain form + みたい (ねているみたい), i-adjective + みたい (おいしいみたい), na-adjective + みたい (げんきみたい)
- らしい: noun + らしい (がくせいらしい), i-adjective + らしい (つよらしい), verb plain form + らしい (ふるらしい = apparently it will fall)

Minimal pairs — notice the difference in nuance:

- この スープは カレーみたいです。(This soup tastes like curry — an IMPRESSION from my own tongue.)
- この みせは カレーやらしいです。(This shop is truly typical of a curry shop — a GENERALLY KNOWN characteristic.)
- かれは びょうきみたいです。(He looks sick — I SAW him looking pale.)
- かれは びょうきらしいです。(Apparently he's sick — I HEARD the news.)

This lesson's five kanji: 暖 (あたたかい = warm), 涼 (すずしい = cool), 霧 (きり = fog), 涙 (なみだ = tears), 汗 (あせ = sweat).

## Example sentences

1. きょうは まるで はるみたいに あたたかいです。(Today is warm, just like spring.)
2. あの ひとは せんせいみたいです。めがねを かけています。(That person looks like a teacher. They're wearing glasses.)
3. さくらの はなは はるらしいですね。(Sakura blossoms are truly typical of spring.)
4. うわさでは、あしたは あめが ふるらしいです。(According to rumors, apparently it will rain tomorrow.)
5. Budiは あせを かいて、つかれているみたいです。(Budi is sweating; he looks tired.)
6. きりが でて、やまが みえません。ふゆみたいです。(Fog has rolled in and the mountains are invisible. It's like winter.)
7. かれの へやは ごみばこみたいです。そうじしましょう。(His room is like a trash can. Let's clean it.)
8. えいがを みて、なみだが でました。かんどうしました。(Watching the movie, my tears fell. I was moved.)

## Practice

1. Choose for this context (you see her yawning): かのじょは（ねむいみたい / ねむいらしい）です。
2. Choose for this context (a general characteristic): にほんは さくらが（ゆうめいみたい / ゆうめいらしい）です。
3. Change: ともだち + みたい → ______
4. Change: がくせい + らしい (typical-of meaning) → ______
5. Translate into Japanese: "It feels like being at home." (at home = うちに いる)
6. Choose for this context (heard from a friend): あした テストが（あるみたい / あるらしい）です。
7. Fix for an impression from your own tongue: この りょうりは おいしいらしいです → ______
8. Make a minimal pair: write two sentences about Sinta being "sick-looking" — the impression version and the hearsay version.

## Tips / key patterns

- みたい = subjective ("to me"), first らしい = objective/general ("truly typical"), second らしい = hearsay.
- Don't use みたい for established general facts: "さくらは はるらしい" ✓, "さくらは はるみたい" ✗.
- Hearsay らしい is similar to 〜そうだ (L32), but らしい is more casual and often used for gossip.
- Polite form: 〜みたいです / 〜らしいです.

## Summary

- 〜みたい: subjective impression from your own observation ("looks like…").
- 〜らしい: (1) widely known characteristics; (2) hearsay "apparently".
- Minimal pairs are the key to telling them apart — practice until it's reflexive.
- Kanji: 暖 (あたたかい), 涼 (すずしい), 霧 (きり), 涙 (なみだ), 汗 (あせ).
