## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use the 〜てくれる pattern to say that someone else did a kindness for you, and you can explain how its direction differs from 〜てあげる.

## Explanation

The pattern **〜てくれる** means "someone else does a kindness for me". To form it: **verb in て form + くれる**.

- おしえる → おしえて → おしえてくれる (to teach me)
- つくる → つくって → つくってくれる (to make for me)
- てつだう → てつだって → てつだってくれる (to help me)

Here is the contrast you must master:

- 〜てあげる: direction **me → someone else** (I'm the one doing the kindness).
- 〜てくれる: direction **someone else → me** (I'm the one receiving the kindness).

The sentence pattern is: Aが わたしに 〜てくれます. Because くれる always means "give to me", this pattern is often used to say thank you.

This lesson's two kanji: 参 (まいる), 存 (ぞんじる) — both humble vocabulary for the keigo lessons.

## Example sentences

1. シンタさんが わたしに にほんごを おしえて くれました。(Sinta taught me Japanese.)
2. ブディさんが わたしを えきまで おくって くれました。(Budi saw me off to the station.)
3. ともだちが ケーキを つくって くれました。(My friend made a cake for me.)
4. あねが しゅくだいを てつだって くれました。(My older sister helped with my homework.)
5. ちちが こわれた とけいを なおして くれました。(My father fixed the broken clock for me.)
6. せんせいが じしょを かして くれました。(The teacher lent me a dictionary.)
7. おばさんが おいしい りょうりを つくって くれました。(My aunt cooked delicious food for me.)
8. タクシーの うんてんしゅさんが にもつを はこんで くれました。(The taxi driver carried my luggage for me.)

## Tips / key patterns

- Common mistake: mixing up the directions. Remember the anchor: あげる = I give, くれる = I receive.
- 〜てくれる often appears in expressions of thanks: てつだって くれて、ありがとう。
- The receiver is almost always me or my group; it doesn't always have to be written, but it can be marked with に.
- In the JLPT, direction questions (who gives to whom) are a classic trap. Read the sentence subject carefully.

## Summary

- 〜てくれる = verb in て form + くれる; it means someone else does a kindness for me.
- The direction is the opposite of 〜てあげる: あげる = me → someone else, くれる = someone else → me.
- Often used for saying thank you and telling about others' kindnesses.
- Kanji: 参 (まいる = to come or go humbly), 存 (ぞんじる = to know humbly).
