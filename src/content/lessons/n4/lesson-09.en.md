## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you understand the basic concept of keigo: sonkeigo raises the listener, while kenjougo lowers yourself. You can determine the direction in a sentence.

## Explanation

Keigo (けいご) is polite Japanese language that governs how you raise or lower people through word choice. There are two main concepts:

**Sonkeigo (そんけいご)** — honorific language. Used to **raise the listener or their superiors**: customers, bosses, teachers. Example: いらっしゃる is the honorific form of くる (to come).

**Kenjougo (けんじょうご)** — humble language. Used to **lower yourself or your own group** before the listener. Example: もうす is the humble form of いう (to say).

The key is direction: sonkeigo is never used for yourself, and kenjougo is never used for a superior. Getting the direction wrong is the same as being impolite.

Some of the most common special verbs, at a glance (details in M6):

- いらっしゃる (honorific): to come, go, be — for the listener.
- もうす (humble): to say — for yourself.
- 伺う (humble): to visit, ask — for yourself.
- 致す (humble): to do — for yourself.

This lesson's three kanji: 伺 (うかがう = to visit or ask humbly), 致 (いたす = to do humbly), 謙 (けん, as in けんきょ = humility).

## Example sentences

1. しゃちょうが いらっしゃいました。(The president has come.)
2. わたしは あした とうきょうの かいしゃに 伺います。(Tomorrow I will visit the company in Tokyo.)
3. ぶちょうは もう いらっしゃいますか。(Is the section manager here yet?)
4. わたしは たなかと もうします。(My name is Tanaka.)
5. せんせいが きのう いらっしゃいました。(The teacher came yesterday.)
6. わたしが ごあんない 致します。(I will show you the way.)
7. おきゃくさまから めいしを いただきました。(I received a business card from the customer.)
8. わたくしが あした でんわを いたします。(I will be the one to call tomorrow.)

8. Kenjougo is used to lower (oneself / a superior) → ______

### Answer key

1. そんけいご
2. けんじょうご
3. けんじょうご
4. the listener
5. しゃちょうが いらっしゃいました。
6. もうします
7. しゃちょうが もうす (もうす is only for oneself)
8. oneself

## Tips / key patterns

- The most fatal mistake: using a humble word for a superior or an honorific word for yourself. Always ask first: who is being raised?
- いただく is the humble form of もらう; it's used when receiving something from a customer or a superior.
- In JLPT N4, keigo questions often only test direction: choose the word with the correct direction.

## Summary

- Sonkeigo raises the listener or their superiors; kenjougo lowers yourself.
- Common examples: いらっしゃる (honorific), もうす・伺う・致す (humble).
- Don't mix up the direction: honorific for them, humble for me.
- Details on forming keigo patterns follow in M6.
- Kanji: 伺 (うかがう), 致 (いたす), 謙 (けん, as in けんきょ = humility).
