## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can change group 2 (ichidan) verbs into the potential form with the 〜られる pattern to express "can do something". This is the foundation before the group 1 potential form in the next lesson.

## Explanation

The potential form expresses ability: "can ...". For group 2 (ichidan) verbs, the pattern is very easy: **drop る, add られる**.

- たべる → たべられる (can eat)
- みる → みられる (can see)
- ねる → ねられる (can sleep)
- おきる → おきられる (can wake up)
- かりる → かりられる (can borrow)

To express ability, the most natural particle is **が**, not を. Compare: さしみを たべる (eating sashimi — an ordinary action) becomes さしみが たべられる (can eat sashimi — ability).

A small note: the particle は is read わ, not は.

A quick look at the passive form: the passive uses exactly the same pattern (〜られる), but it means "to be ...-ed" (for example たべられる = is eaten), while the potential means "can...". Tell them apart from the sentence context. The passive is covered thoroughly in M5.

This lesson's three kanji: 残 (のこる = to remain), 辞 (やめる = to quit a job), 始 (はじめる = to start).

## Example sentences

1. シンタさんは からい さしみが たべられます。(Sinta can eat spicy sashimi.)
2. ブディさんは あたらしい うたを すぐ おぼえられます。(Budi can memorize new songs right away.)
3. かのじょは ともだちの じてんしゃが かりられます。(She can borrow her friend's bicycle.)
4. あには まいにち あさ 6じに おきられます。(My older brother can wake up at 6 in the morning every day.)
5. ちちは おもい ドアを ひとりで あけられます。(My father can open the heavy door by himself.)
6. わたしは あの ひとの でんわばんごうが わすれられません。(I can't forget that person's phone number.)
7. さとうさんは にほんごの しんぶんの かんじが しらべられます。(Satou can look up the kanji in a Japanese newspaper.)
8. きのうは とても つかれたので、はやく ねられました。(Yesterday I was very tired, so I could fall asleep earlier.)

## Tips / key patterns

- Common mistake: attaching れる directly to the ます form (たべます → たべますれる). The correct pattern always starts from the dictionary form: drop る, add られる.
- For ability, make it a habit to use が: さしみが たべられます.
- Negative form: たべられません (can't eat). Past form: たべられました.
- In casual conversation, 〜られる is often shortened to 〜れる (for example たべれる), but in the JLPT and formal situations use the full form.

## Summary

- Group 2 potential: drop る + られる (たべる → たべられる).
- It means "can ..."; the most natural ability particle is が.
- The particle は is read わ.
- The passive uses the same pattern (〜られる) with the meaning "to be ...-ed"; details in M5.
- Kanji: 残 (のこる = to remain), 辞 (やめる = to quit a job), 始 (はじめる = to start).
