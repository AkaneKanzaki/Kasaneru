## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you understand the suffering passive: intransitive verbs turned passive in the pattern わたしは X に V-passive to express "I was negatively affected by that event".

## Explanation

The suffering passive is used for events that harm the speaker, and its hallmark is: **even intransitive verbs can be made passive**. The pattern is **わたしは X に V-passive**, meaning "I suffered because X did V". English has no direct equivalent; the closest analogy is a construction like "got caught in the rain" or "had my bike stolen".

Formation examples from intransitive verbs:

- ふる (rain falls) → ふられる: あめに ふられた = got caught in the rain.
- なく (to cry) → なかれる: あかちゃんに なかれた = bothered by the baby's crying.
- にげる (to run away) → にげられる: ともだちに にげられた = left behind when a friend ran away.
- しぬ (to die) → しなれる: ちちに しなれた = left behind by father's death.

The key is the nuance of "I am the one who lost out", different from the neutral passive in L21. The subject is almost always "I" and may be omitted. Transitive verbs can also carry the nuance of loss: ともだちに うたがわれた ("I was suspected by a friend").

The eight kanji of this lesson: 害 (がい = harm), 被 (ひ = to receive — in 被害), 課 (かす = to assign a task), 巻 (まく = to roll up), 慣 (なれる = to get used to), 技 (わざ = technique), 疑 (うたがう = suspicion), 居 (いる = to exist).

## Example sentences

1. きのう あめに ふられた。(Yesterday I got caught in the rain.)
2. あかちゃんに よるじゅう なかれた。(The baby's crying bothered me all night.)
3. ともだちに にげられた。(My friend ran away and left me behind.)
4. わたしは ともだちに 疑(うたが)われた。(I was suspected by a friend.)
5. かれは けんかに 巻(ま)きこまれた。(He got dragged into a fight.)
6. たいふうで まちに おおきな 被害(ひがい)が でた。(The typhoon caused great damage to the town.)
7. わたしは にほんの せいかつに もう 慣(な)れた。(I have already gotten used to life in Japan.)
8. かれは けんどうの 技(わざ)を せんせいに ほめられた。(His kendo technique was praised by the teacher.)

## Tips / key patterns

- Only the suffering passive can use intransitive verbs. This is its main distinction from the ordinary passive in L21.
- The subject is almost always the speaker ("I") and is often omitted. The focus is on X に, the source of the harm.
- さかなを たべられた ("someone ate my fish") is indeed transitive, but carries the nuance of loss, so it counts as a suffering passive.
- The "got caught in" analogy doesn't always fit; sometimes a free translation ("my friend ran away and left me") is more natural.

## Summary

- Pattern: わたしは X に V-passive = "I suffered because X did V".
- Intransitive verbs can be made passive: ふられる, なかれる, にげられる, しなれる.
- The required nuance: harm to the speaker. Without this nuance, it isn't a suffering passive.
- English analogy: constructions like "got caught in the rain", "had something stolen".
