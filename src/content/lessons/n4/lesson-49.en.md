## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use the third mock as calibration: compare your in-app mock results with the official JLPT sample questions, read the score gap per section, and decide: ready, or one more review round needed.

## Explanation

**Official anchor** = the official N4 sample questions from the JLPT site (jlpt.jp). This is the only material whose difficulty is GUARANTEED to match the real exam. If your performance on the official samples aligns with your app mocks, your app's estimated score can be trusted.

**How to calibrate (2 days):**

1. **Day 1:** work through the official sample questions WITHOUT a timer. Record your score per section (estimated) + mark wrong/uncertain questions.
2. **Day 2:** run mock #3 in Real mode in the app (115 minutes, L48 rules).
3. **Compare per section**, not the totals:

| Section | Official sample | App mock | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| LK + Reading | .../120 | .../120 | ±... |
| Listening | .../60 | .../60 | ±... |

**Reading the gap:** gap ≤±10% and everything above the threshold → VALID. Mock much higher → trust the official sample. Official sample much higher → likely fatigue during the mock; check your clinic table. One section crashes in one source but is safe in the other → that section is NOT STABLE yet; it needs a dedicated review round.

## Example sentences

The correct reading:

> "Official sample: LK + Reading 55/120, Listening 28/60 (estimated). Mock: 62/120 and 33/60. Gap <10%, everything above the threshold, second consecutive passing mock. Conclusion: READY. For the next week just fine-tuning: 15 minutes of vocabulary drills per day."

The wrong reading (don't copy this):

> "Total 95/180, I passed! Although Listening on the official sample was only 14/60." — One section below the threshold = not ready yet, even if the total passes.

## Practice

For each scenario, decide: READY or NEEDS 1 REVIEW ROUND + the reason.

1. Official sample: 48/120 and 25/60. Mock-3: 52/120 and 30/60. Previous mock-2 also passed.
2. Official sample: 70/120 and 35/60. Mock-3: 45/120 and 22/60 — done at 11 PM after overtime work.
3. Official sample: 58/120 and 16/60. Mock-3: 60/120 and 32/60.

### Answer key

1. READY — gap <10%, everything above the threshold, two consecutive passing mocks.
2. INVESTIGATE FIRST — big gap + poor conditions. Repeat the crashed section when fresh; if the score recovers, the problem is stamina, not material.
3. NEEDS 1 REVIEW ROUND — Listening on the official sample (16/60) is below the threshold (±19/60). Action: 1 week of focused listening (L43–L44 + clinic table), recalibrate the Listening section.

## Tips / key patterns

- Do the official sample HONESTLY: no dictionary, no peeking at explanations. A cheated anchor anchors nothing.
- Calibrate at most once a week — the official samples are limited in number.
- Record the date of each calibration; an upward trend over 3 weeks is more convincing than one high score.
- Compare PER SECTION — JLPT failure almost always happens in one section.

## Summary

- Official anchor = the N4 sample questions on jlpt.jp; difficulty guaranteed.
- Calibration: official sample vs mock-3 → compare PER SECTION.
- A ±10% gap + everything above the threshold = valid.
- One section below the threshold on the official sample = not ready yet.
- Both are ESTIMATES; official scores use statistical scaling.
