## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can assess your own readiness with an honest checklist — two consecutive mocks meeting the thresholds, your remaining list of weak words and kanji, proven time discipline — and you have a concrete test-day plan: logistics, the night before, the morning, and a mental strategy.

## Explanation

**Readiness checklist.** You're ready if all of these are met:

1. Two consecutive Real-mode mocks: estimated total ≥95/180 and each section above the estimated threshold.
2. Your list of weak words and kanji is down to a few, and all are in the SRS deck.
3. Proven time discipline: never ran out of time in the last two mocks.
4. Your personal error patterns (from L47) already have running repair actions.
5. Physical condition maintained: enough sleep in the last week, no staying up late.

If one point isn't met yet, that's your last week's work map.

**Test-day plan.** Logistics: prepare your もちもの the night before (じゅけんひょう, ID, pencils, eraser, watch, drinking water), and do a したみ of the route to the かいじょう at least a day before. The night before: stop studying at nine and go to bed early — staying up late instead destroys the next day's 140 minutes. Morning: wake up with 余裕, two hours before leaving, a 朝食 as usual, arrive 30 minutes early.

**Mental strategy.** Being nervous is normal — it means you care. Prepare a 2-minute routine before each section: sit up straight, three deep breaths, read the first question. And remember: one bad question must not punish the remaining 139 minutes. Forgive yourself, move to the next question.

## Example sentences

Wrong example: staying up until 2 AM memorizing vocabulary, arriving just in time, then panicking in the first 10 minutes.

## Practice

1. Name the five readiness-checklist points. Which point do you think is hardest to meet? Why?
2. Why is staying up late the night before the exam strictly forbidden in this guide?
3. What もちもの must be prepared the night before?
4. Explain the "forgiveness rule" and when it is used.
5. Write your own morning timeline from waking up to arriving at the かいじょう.

### Answer key

1. Two consecutive mocks meeting the thresholds; the weak word/kanji list down to a few and in SRS; proven time discipline; error patterns with running actions; physical condition maintained.
2. Because staying up late destroys concentration for the 140 minutes — the loss is bigger than the extra material crammed.
3. じゅけんひょう, ID, pencils, eraser, watch, drinking water.
4. One bad question must not punish the remaining time: forgive yourself and move to the next question.
5. Personal answer; the correct one includes 余裕 of time (wake up 2 hours before leaving, arrive 30 minutes early) and a usual 朝食.

## Tips / key patterns

- Don't study new material on H-1; just review what you've mastered so your confidence rises.
- When nerves peak, focus on your breathing and the question in front of you, not the final result.

## Summary

- Ready = two consecutive mocks meeting the thresholds + controlled weak list + proven time discipline + maintained physical condition.
- H-1: stop studying at 21.00, prepare もちもの, sleep early.
- Test day: wake up with 余裕, usual 朝食, arrive 30 minutes early, 2-minute routine each section.
- One bad question doesn't punish the remaining 139 minutes: forgive yourself, move on.
- Kanji checklist: 戒（戒める・いましめる）・赦（赦す・ゆるす）。

## Tasks & SRS notes

Write your own test-day timeline and post it somewhere visible. Check off each readiness-checklist point; unmet ones become your last week's work map. Add to your SRS deck: 戒める、赦す、もちもの、じゅけんひょう、かいじょう、したみ、余裕、きんちょう、朝食、早寝. Kanji checklist: write each kanji with its reading three times without looking at your notes. — See you at the finish line. You're ready.
