## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can group all your mock errors into four strands (ぶんぽう・ごい・どっかい・ちょうかい), find recurring patterns, and write specific repair actions for each category — not just "study more".

## Explanation

The error clinic works in three steps: collect, group, repair.

**1. Collect.** Take the logs from L45 and L46. Separate "don't know" (material not yet mastered) from "knew it but got it wrong" (carelessness, ran out of time, fell for a distractor).

**2. Group by strand.** Put each error into the table below. A category that appears three or more times is a pattern — and patterns are your main target. Remember: 殆どのごとう come from recurring patterns.

| Strand | Category | Repair action |
|---|---|---|
| ぶんぽう | Similar patterns mixed up (〜ばかりか vs 〜どころか) | Comparison table + 3 sentences per pattern |
| ぶんぽう | Wrong particle (after 〜に対して) | 10-sentence special drill |
| ごい | Similar words (まつ vs もつ) | Write in pairs + distinguishing examples |
| ごい | Wrong collocation (×くすりを食べる) | Memorize as a phrase |
| どっかい | Detail mistaken for main idea | Mark the first sentence of each paragraph |
| どっかい | A character's opinion mistaken for fact | Practice fact-vs-opinion labeling |
| どっかい | Ran out of time | Timed drills, cut 30 seconds each session |
| ちょうかい | Nuance distractor (いいね mistaken for agreement) | Practice marking negations (L44) |
| ちょうかい | Numbers not noted | Write down all numbers while listening |

**3. Repair with priority.** Rank the categories by (frequency × ease of repair); work first on what appears often AND is easy to fix.

## Example sentences

Log: どっかい | main idea | picking a detail | action: write the main idea in one sentence before looking at the options.
Log: ちょうかい | distractor | いいね mistaken for agreement | action: collect 10 soft-refusal phrases.

## Practice

1. Group: (a) mixing up 予約する and よしゅうする, (b) picking an example as the main idea, (c) 火曜日 thought to be the answer though it was cancelled, (d) 〜ものだ written as 〜ことだ.
2. Take one of your real errors and fill in: strand | category | cause | action.
3. Three categories: A (6x, easy), B (2x, hard), C (5x, medium). Determine the working order and the reason.
4. What is the difference in handling "don't know" vs "knew it but got it wrong"?
5. Write a 7-day plan for your first priority category (15 minutes/day).

### Answer key

1. (a) ごい | similar words; (b) どっかい | detail mistaken for main idea; (c) ちょうかい | cancelled info; (d) ぶんぽう | similar patterns mixed up.
2. Personal answer; the correct one contains all four columns with a specific action.
3. Order: A → C → B. Prioritize what's frequent and easy to fix because the score rises fastest.
4. "Don't know" → learn the material from scratch. "Knew it but got it wrong" → fix the process (pacing, question-reading technique, timed drills).
5. Answers vary; the correct one: a specific 15-minute daily target, e.g. days 1–2 comparison table, days 3–5 sentence drills, days 6–7 retest.

## Tips / key patterns

- Don't fix everything at once — at most two categories per week.
- Even on a 頗る good day, careless errors can appear; it's greatly regrettable (甚だざんねん) if they're not recorded.

## Summary

- Three steps: collect the logs, group by strand, repair with priority.
- Distinguish "don't know" (learn the material) vs "knew it but got it wrong" (fix the process).
- Priority = frequency × ease; at most two categories per week.
- Kanji checklist (strokes): 殆（殆ど・ほとんど）・頗（頗る・すこぶる）・甚（甚だ・はなはだ）。
