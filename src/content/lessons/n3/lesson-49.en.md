## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you can use the official JLPT sample question set as an anchor (benchmark): running the third mock in Real mode, comparing its results against official question difficulty, and analyzing the gaps so your ability estimate is calibrated before test day.

## Explanation

**The official anchor** is the set of official JLPT sample questions used as the difficulty benchmark. The app's question bank trains your volume and speed, but only official questions show the true standard.

**How to compare.** Don't compare raw scores — compare patterns. Work through one official sample set without strict timing, then note: which question types felt much harder than the question bank? Where is the gap most conspicuous? Those are your real gaps.

**Gap analysis.** Make a table: area | mock 3 result | impression vs official questions | action. Gaps you ignore resurface on test day; gaps you write down shrink.

**Estimate calibration.** After three mocks, you have trend data: rising, stagnant, or falling. If the last two mocks stagnated in one section, don't hope for a miracle on test day — raise your baseline in that section by returning to specific drills, not by adding new mocks, so you don't have to learn the hard way (懲りる) from the same mistakes.

## Example sentences

| Area | Mock 3 (estimate) | Vs official questions | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| げんごちしき | 34/60 | On par | Maintain, light drills |
| どっかい | 28/60 | Official (texts) longer | Drill L41 3 minutes daily |
| ちょうかい | 30/60 | Distractors subtler | Drill L44 + note negations |

Example of a good conclusion: "My total score is 96 — above the threshold. But どっかい lags far behind the official standard, so the last week focuses on reading speed, not adding more mocks."

## Practice

1. What is the function of the "official anchor" in your preparation? Why is the question bank alone not enough?
2. Why compare patterns, not raw scores?
3. Your mock 3 result: total 98, どっかい 26/60, and the official どっかい questions felt much harder. What is your analysis and your action?
4. Your last two mocks stagnated in ちょうかい. What should be done in the last week?
5. Write a one-line calibration conclusion for your own situation (or an example).

### Answer key

1. The official anchor is the benchmark of true difficulty. The question bank trains volume and speed, but the official standard is only visible in official questions.
2. Because the scale and number of questions may differ; what determines readiness is the kind of difficulty that still defeats you, not the numbers.
3. Analysis: total above the threshold but どっかい below the official standard → not safe yet. Action: focus on daily reading-speed drills, not adding new mocks.
4. Return to specific drills (L43–L44) to raise the baseline; adding mocks without fixing patterns only repeats the same result.
5. Example: "Total 96, above the threshold, but the ちょうかい margin is thin and どっかい is below the official standard — last week: speed-reading drills + listening distractors."

## Tips / key patterns

- Work the official sample questions in relaxed conditions first (no timer) — the goal is to recognize the standard, not to test your nerve.
- One gap written down and worked on beats ten gaps only worried about.
- If all trends are rising, reward yourself.
