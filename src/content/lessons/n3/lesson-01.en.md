## Lesson goals

After this lesson, you will be able to use two advanced passive patterns that frequently appear on the exam: the suffering passive (〜めいわくのうけみ) — the "wallet stolen / cake eaten by someone / caught in the rain" pattern that expresses loss — and the causative-passive, the "made to / forced to" pattern like 行かせられる.

## Explanation

**1. The suffering passive.** In N4 you learned the ordinary passive (この本は 多くの人に 読まれている). The suffering passive is formed the same way, but its meaning is: the speaker **suffers** from someone else's action. Its hallmarks: the doer is marked with に, and often an object belonging to the speaker is marked with を.

- さいふを 盗まれる (my wallet was stolen)
- ケーキを 食べられる (someone ate my cake — and I'm upset about it)
- 雨に ふられる (caught in the rain)

The third example has no を, but the meaning is still "suffering". Tell it apart from the ordinary passive by context: if the event harms the speaker, it's the suffering passive.

**2. The causative-passive.** A combination of the causative (〜させる) + passive (〜られる), meaning "made to / forced to" with a burdened nuance.

- 行く → 行かせる → 行かせられる (made to go)
- 食べる → 食べさせる → 食べさせられる (forced to eat)
- する → させる → させられる (made to do)
- 来る → 来させる → 来させられる (made to come)
- まつ → またせる → またせられる (made to wait)

Don't attach られる directly to the dictionary form. Always pass through the causative form first.

The eight kanji of this lesson: 権 (けん = rights, e.g. 権利), 利 (り = benefit, e.g. りよう), 義 (ぎ = moral obligation, e.g. 義務), 務 (む = duty), 制 (せい = system, e.g. 制度), 度 (ど = degree), 革 (かく = reform, e.g. 革命), 命 (めい = orders, life, e.g. めいれい, いのち).

## Example sentences

1. ブディさんは 電車の 中で さいふを 盗まれました。(Budi had his wallet stolen inside the train.)
2. シンタさんは おとうとに ケーキを 食べられました。(Sinta's cake was eaten by her younger brother, and Sinta was upset.)
3. ちちは まいばん となりの 人に 大きな おとで おんがくを 聞かれました。(Every night, my father was bothered because the neighbor played music loudly.)
4. わたしは ははに まいあさ 6時に 起きさせられます。(I am made to wake up at 6 every morning by my mother.)
5. あには 会社で まいにち ざんぎょうを させられます。(My older brother is made to work overtime every day at the office.)
6. 子どもの とき、わたしは ははに やさいを 食べさせられました。(When I was a child, I was forced to eat vegetables by my mother.)
7. デートの 日に 雨に ふられて、とても 困りました。(Caught in the rain on the day of my date, I was in great trouble.)
8. いもうとは ははに へやの そうじを させられました。(My younger sister was forced to clean her room by my mother.)

## Tips / key patterns

- For group-2 verbs, the passive and potential forms are the same (食べられる). Tell them apart by context: a doer with に + a nuance of loss = passive; talking about ability with が = potential.
- The causative-passive always takes two steps: dictionary → 〜させる → 〜させられる. Don't skip the causative step.
- The doer takes に; one's own belongings take を.

## Summary

- Suffering passive: ordinary passive form, meaning "suffering a loss" (さいふを 盗まれた, 雨に ふられた).
- Causative-passive: 〜させる + られる = "made to / forced to" (行かせられる, 食べさせられる, させられる, 来させられる).
- Don't skip the causative step; the doer takes に.
- Kanji: 権 (rights), 利 (benefit), 義 (moral obligation), 務 (duty), 制 (system), 度 (degree), 革 (reform), 命 (orders, life).
